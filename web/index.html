An assistant that buys, judges, and pays.

This is the other side of the transaction. A personal assistant acting for a human under a standing mandate — the kind a calendar gives you: the investor meeting is at six, and nobody wants to be woken up to approve a one-dollar purchase. So it places the order, judges what comes back, and settles the invoice itself. Two repositories, two agents, no human at either end.

orders a verdict, judges it, and pays — every step it took

What it never does
ask a human. The mandate was granted once, with a ceiling, and it spends inside it

It decides its own payments

Broker gets no say in this. The client evaluates the deliverable against its own criteria and can decline — an empty answer, a price above the mandate, or expert rates charged for an agent-only guess are all refused.

—

The boundary, and how it is enforced

The payer holds the payment credential; Broker holds a read-only key and can only observe that money arrived. A supplier who can charge you at will is not a counterparty, it is a subscription.

client → sk_test_ (settles) · Broker → rk_ (reads) · settlement refuses any key that is not a test key

The same agent also runs an adversarial lane against Broker, over the real wire, from an identity that is not inside it. An attack Broker survives is only evidence if the attacker was never inside.
